## Review

This is a workable three-day upper-body specialization layout. Chest and back are each trained twice per cycle, with recovery days between sessions, and the saved progression rule is sensible: usually retain one or two repetitions in reserve and increase load after all prescribed sets reach the top of the range with clean technique. Whether the workload is appropriate depends on your performance and recovery.

1. **Lower body is absent.** If this is your entire lifting routine, it leaves quads, hamstrings, glutes, and calves without programmed resistance work. If you train legs separately, that changes the assessment. For general whole-body development, include knee-dominant and hip-hinge or leg-curl work across the week, reallocating some upper-body sets if necessary. Current [ACSM guidance](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/) recommends engaging all major muscle groups, with at least two weekly resistance-training sessions.
2. **Pull day has potentially redundant rowing.** It contains three pulldown sets plus six cable-row sets, followed by three reverse-pec-deck sets. Keep both rows if they serve deliberately different purposes and you recover well; otherwise, one row variation is an obvious place to trim two or three sets. More rowing is not automatically harmful, but it uses time and elbow-flexor capacity.
3. **Arms are described as a priority but come late.** On C, curls follow nine compound pulling sets and three reverse-pec-deck sets. Consider placing one curl earlier, after the first main pull, if arm performance is the priority; accept that later pulling performance may change. Exercise order tends to favor strength gains in exercises performed earlier; a [meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32077380/) did not establish a hypertrophy advantage from one order.
4. **Shoulder and arm work already includes compound overlap.** Seven lateral-raise sets, five shoulder-press sets, seven curl sets, and five triceps-focused sets are a credible starting allocation alongside pressing and pulling. Do not automatically add sets merely to reach a universal ten-set quota. ACSM's approximately ten-set hypertrophy guidance is a broad reference, not proof that eight chest sets or five isolation sets cannot produce growth.
5. **Abs already receive nine sets.** Keep that if they progress and recover. If session length is a problem, two sets per workout would free three weekly sets for another priority. This is a practical option, not a claim that nine sets is excessive.
6. **Rest should preserve set quality.** The 120–130-second pressing rests are a reasonable starting point. If reps or technique deteriorate because you are not ready, allow roughly two to three minutes on demanding compounds. Resting 60 seconds on single-arm rows may be short depending on whether you rest after both sides. A [2024 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39205815/) found growth across different rest lengths, with a small overall advantage for longer rests.
